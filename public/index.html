The 5 Color-Coded Segregation Streams

Strict categorisation under Schedule I of the 2016 Rules. Proper colour coding prevents mixing and guarantees valid treatment pathways. Includes general solid waste for complete facility compliance.

BLACK BAG CAT-E (General)

General Solid Waste

Non-hazardous solid waste from general areas: food waste, packaging, paper, office waste, garden waste. Does not include infectious, chemical, or sharps waste.

CONTAINER SPECIFICATION

Black opaque plastic bags, securely tied at point of generation.

REQUIRED TREATMENT

Municipal solid waste disposal via authorised local body collection.

YELLOW BAG CAT-A (Non-Chlorinated)

Anatomical & Soiled Waste

Human/animal anatomical tissues, organs, body parts, fetus. Soiled items contaminated with blood or body fluids — dressings, plaster casts, cotton swabs. Expired medicines, chemotherapy drugs, lab cultures.

CONTAINER SPECIFICATION

Non-chlorinated yellow plastic bags.

REQUIRED TREATMENT

Incineration or Plasma Pyrolysis or deep burial.

RED BAG CAT-B (Recyclable)

Contaminated Plastics

Disposable plastic items: tubing, IV bottles and sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes without needles, and other recyclable contaminated plastic waste.

CONTAINER SPECIFICATION

Non-chlorinated red plastic bags / containers.

REQUIRED TREATMENT

Autoclaving / microwaving / hydroclaving followed by shredding.

WHITE BOX CAT-C (Sharps)

Sharps & Metals

Needles, syringes with fixed needles, scalpels, blades, any contaminated sharp metal object. Both used and discarded sharps that can cause puncture or cut injuries.

CONTAINER SPECIFICATION

Translucent, puncture-proof, tamper-proof, leak-proof container.

REQUIRED TREATMENT

Autoclaving or Dry Heat Sterilization followed by shredding or mutilation.

BLUE BOX CAT-D (Glassware)

Glassware & Implants

Broken or discarded glass including medicine vials, ampoules (except those contaminated with cytotoxic drugs), glass bottles. Also metallic body implants.

CONTAINER SPECIFICATION

Cardboard box with blue marking.

REQUIRED TREATMENT

Disinfection by soaking in Sodium Hypochlorite, then autoclaving or microwaving, sent for recycling.

Schedule I — Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (India). All healthcare facilities must comply. Mixing categories is a punishable offence.

DAILY PRACTICE, NOT JUST PAPERWORK

Hospital & Waste Collector Responsibilities

Practical, ward-level instructions for the two teams who keep waste segregation and collection safe.

Hospitals & Clinics

Segregate right at the source

  • → Segregate waste into the correct colour-coded bag or box the moment it's generated — never sort it later.
  • → Keep sharps in a rigid, puncture-proof container at all times; never recap or bend used needles.
  • → Store filled bags in a covered, pest-proof holding area, away from patient care zones, until pickup.
  • → Log every day's dispatched and used/segregated items in the Hospital Login logbook so gaps get caught early.
  • → Have the waste management head verify each collector's pickup before it's marked complete.

Waste Collectors & Disposers

Collect, don't mix

  • → Check that each bag or box is sealed, correctly colour-coded, and not leaking before you accept it.
  • → Never combine categories in one bin during transport — sharps, soiled, plastics, and glass stay apart.
  • → Wear gloves and closed footwear when handling any bag, and wash hands immediately after.
  • → Photograph the waste with its disposal location in the Collector Login as proof of pickup.
  • → Deliver only to the disposal or treatment point assigned to that hospital, and wait for verification.
